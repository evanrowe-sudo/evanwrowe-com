Warm-up: same championship game, three people

Rank them 1 (worst) to 3. Where exactly does the line get crossed?

The Fixer

A starting basketball player promises a gambler his team still wins, but he'll miss two late free throws so they win by less than the 10-point spread.

The Spot-Rigger

A tennis player tanks the first set on purpose to move the live odds, then plays straight and wins. The result is real. The in-game market wasn't.

The Intel Operator

A janitor sees the starting QB limping and throwing up at a closed practice. He texts a pro bettor before the injury report comes out.

How schemes actually get caught

Alabama baseball, 2023. Not clever detective work. A very large bet, placed in person, on a game nobody cared about, and the casino flagged it.

Same tell as insider trading: a huge buy in Nvidia disappears into the volume; the same money in a penny stock trips every alarm. That's why books keep limits low on small games and watch them hardest.

Questions

  1. The sport. Which sports are easiest to corrupt? Is singles tennis or golf more foolproof than football, with no teammates to bring in and keep quiet?
  2. Prop bets. You can move one player's stat line and leave the score alone. Does that even count as fixing?
  3. Intel vs. cheating. Someone learns something true and bets on it. Criminal, or just early?
  4. Legalization. What changes when a space that already had a black market goes legal and advertised?

The Red Team: your panel's final project

1 · The scheme

You're on the inside. Move money on information nobody else has, in today's market, without tripping anything.

2 · Where it falls apart

Limits, monitoring, records at every legal book. What are the workarounds, and which ones leave a trace anyway?

3 · Switch sides

Now you're the reporter. What records do you pull, which sources do you work, what does the pattern look like in the line?

It's an analysis of how integrity fails, not a how-to. Real, settled cases are fair game. No accusing real people of things they haven't been found guilty of.
