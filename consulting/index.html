Teaching, online courses and AI

I built my own AI course system. I can show your faculty how.

I've taught as instructor of record for twenty years, now at three schools at once. To run that load, I built an AI course operations system and course designer that I think is best in class. It runs my courses every day.

Online & asynchronous

Course builds

Due dates, grade weights, an accessible course calendar and module pages, built and pushed to Canvas in one pass. A daily check catches broken links, unpublished pages and quiz questions about material the course never covered, before students run into them.

Video

Live lectures into finished videos

Record a class on a phone, on Zoom, or in any other format. The system transcribes it, matches the audio to the slides, cuts on exact words, adds the clips shown in the room, and burns in captions. The captions count toward accessibility too.

Workshops

Faculty training

Half-day or full-day sessions on building tools fitted to your own courses, and on running AI in class without a rulebook that goes stale by midterm.

Ask first, then rule

I don't write a fixed AI policy into my syllabi. Students ask before they use a tool, I rule, and the rulings build into precedent over the term. A policy written in August is behind by October.

Don't let a vendor contract pick your tools

In my experience, OpenAI and Anthropic are far ahead of Google's Gemini right now, and that order could change within a year. Faculty and institutions should be able to use whatever works best, in the classroom and in the office. I help colleges write AI guidance that holds up when the tools change.

For presidents and boards of trustees

Spend less on administration and more on teaching

Many college offices spend most of their hours on routine work: building course shells, checking compliance, moving data between systems, answering the same questions, and producing the same reports every term. AI can now do much of that work. My own system already handles course setup, accessibility checks and daily course reviews that colleges usually staff.

I review how your administrative offices spend their time, show which work AI can take on today, and estimate what the institution would save. The aim is a college that puts more of its money into instruction.
